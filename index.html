Help

Frequently asked questions

Is Mammoth Blocker really free?

Yes. Every feature is free for everyone, and it will stay that way. There is no premium version. If you want to help, you can support it on Ko-fi, but nothing is locked behind it.

Does Mammoth collect any of my data?

No. Mammoth has no server, no account system and no analytics. Your settings and counts stay in your browser. Read the privacy policy for the details.

How is Mammoth different from other ad blockers?

Mammoth is small, simple and open. It never lets paid ads through, it shows you every domain it blocks, and it adds a scam check that runs on your device.

Which browsers does it work on?

Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi and other Chrome-based browsers, plus Firefox (version 121 or newer) on desktop.

How does the scam check work?

When you click the button, Mammoth reads the page you're on and looks for common tricks with built-in rules. In Chrome, if your computer supports it, it also asks the browser's own on-device AI for a second opinion. The AI can add a warning but never remove one. Nothing is sent anywhere.

A website looks broken. What do I do?

Open the Mammoth popup and switch it off for that site. The page reloads and works like normal. You can also report the site so we can fix it for everyone.

Will it block ads on YouTube and social media?

Mammoth blocks known ad and tracking networks everywhere, including social pixels. Some big sites serve ads from their own servers, and those can't always be blocked without breaking the site.

Why is the "Annoyances" list off by default?

It hides cookie banners and notification nags. That's great, but it can change how some pages look, so you decide. One switch in Options turns it on.

Why does it ask for an extra permission to count?

The popup shows what was blocked on the current page without any extra permission. A running total and a live number on the toolbar icon need one more browser permission, so that part is off until you turn it on.

Will Mammoth slow down my computer?

No. The browser does the blocking with its own built-in engine, and pages usually get faster because less junk downloads.

Can I use it on my phone?

Not yet. Mammoth is a desktop extension for now. Firefox for Android support is on the list.

How do I remove it?

Right-click the Mammoth icon in your toolbar and pick "Remove from Chrome" (or "Remove Extension" in Firefox). All its settings are deleted with it.
